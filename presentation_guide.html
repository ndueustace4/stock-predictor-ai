Stock Predictor AI

Presentation Preparation Guide  ·  chimatest.online  ·  June 2026

1. Problem Statement

Stock markets generate enormous amounts of data every day — price movements, trading volumes, financial news, economic indicators — yet most individual investors and students have no practical way to make sense of it all.

The Problem

  • Predicting stock direction requires combining multiple data sources at once: price history, technical indicators, news sentiment, and macroeconomic factors
  • Most existing tools either show raw numbers without explanation, or require deep financial expertise to interpret
  • News events (CEO resignations, earnings reports, product launches) can shift a stock's direction within hours — but traditional daily models miss this
  • Even when a model makes a prediction, users have no way to understand why — making it impossible to trust or act on

The Gap This Fills

  • No single accessible tool combines technical analysis + news sentiment + macroeconomic data + plain-English explanation
  • Academic models are tested on historical data but rarely deployed live for real-time use
  • Explainability (understanding why a model predicted something) is often missing from student and research-level projects
  • Most prediction tools only target one time horizon — not four simultaneously

2. What Makes It Different

Multi-Source Data Fusion

Combines price history, technical indicators, FinBERT news sentiment, and macroeconomic data (interest rates, inflation) into a single feature set — most student projects use only one or two sources.

Event-Driven Re-Prediction

GNews is polled every 30 minutes. When breaking news arrives (CEO fired, earnings miss), sentiment is recalculated and the model re-predicts that stock immediately — not the next day.

Competitive Model Training

LightGBM and XGBoost compete for every stock × horizon combination. The best-performing model is automatically selected. This avoids manually choosing one algorithm and hoping it generalises.

SHAP Explainability

Every prediction shows the top 3 factors that drove it — e.g. "RSI was 28 (oversold) and pushed the model toward UP by +0.14". Users understand why, not just what.

4 Simultaneous Horizons

Most tools predict only next-day direction. This system predicts 1 day, 1 week, 1 month, and 1 year simultaneously, with separate trained models for each — giving a complete picture.

AI Chat in Plain English

An AI chatbot (Claude) is injected with live prediction data, sentiment scores, price history, and model accuracy metrics before every response — so answers are grounded in real data, not generic finance advice.

Sentiment vs No-Sentiment Comparison

Two model variants are trained for every stock and horizon. The dashboard shows users whether adding news sentiment actually improves accuracy — making the impact of NLP measurable.

Fully Live Deployed System

Not just a notebook or prototype — a production system running 24/7 on Google Cloud with a real domain (chimatest.online), automated scheduler, serverless database, and CDN-hosted frontend.

7. ML Models — Technical Deep Dive

Two algorithms compete for every stock + horizon: LightGBM and XGBoost.
The winner (by F1 score) is automatically saved. This is called competitive training.

Two variants per model: Without sentiment (price/technical data only) · With sentiment (adds FinBERT news scores)

Total: 10 stocks × 4 horizons × 2 variants = 80 trained models

XGBoost — What It Is and Why It Was Chosen

XGBoost (Extreme Gradient Boosting) is an ensemble machine learning algorithm that builds many small decision trees one after another, where each new tree corrects the mistakes of the previous ones. This process is called gradient boosting.

Why XGBoost for this project:
  • Handles tabular data extremely well — stock features (RSI, MACD, sentiment scores) are structured rows and columns, exactly what XGBoost excels at
  • No feature scaling needed — unlike neural networks, tree-based models don't require normalising inputs, which simplifies the pipeline
  • Built-in regularisation — reg_alpha (L1) and reg_lambda (L2) parameters prevent overfitting on small datasets
  • Class imbalance handling — scale_pos_weight is set to count(DOWN) / count(UP), so the model doesn't just predict UP for everything
  • Industry proven — XGBoost won the majority of Kaggle competitions involving tabular data between 2014–2020; well-studied in quantitative finance
  • Works with TimeSeriesSplit — correctly respects time order during cross-validation so future data never leaks into training

LightGBM — What It Is and Why It Was Chosen

LightGBM (Light Gradient Boosting Machine) is Microsoft's gradient boosting framework. It uses the same boosting principle as XGBoost but with two key differences: it grows trees leaf-wise (deeper, more targeted splits) instead of level-wise, and uses histogram-based binning to speed up training.

Why LightGBM was added as a challenger:
  • Faster training — typically 3–10× faster than XGBoost on the same data, useful when retraining 80 models across 10 stocks
  • Leaf-wise growth — can find more complex patterns in the data, sometimes outperforming XGBoost on the same dataset
  • num_leaves parameter — gives finer control over model complexity than XGBoost's max_depth alone
  • is_unbalance flag — LightGBM's native way to handle UP/DOWN class imbalance, equivalent to XGBoost's scale_pos_weight
  • Neither algorithm is universally better — competitive training lets the data decide which works best per stock and time horizon

How Competitive Training Works (step by step)

StepWhat happens
1. Split dataTime-based split — training data is everything before the cutoff date; test data is everything after. No random shuffling — time order is preserved to avoid leaking future prices into training
2. Hyperparameter searchRandomizedSearchCV tests 25 random combinations of hyperparameters for XGBoost and 25 for LightGBM, using TimeSeriesSplit (3-fold) so each fold still respects time order
3. Train bothBest XGBoost config trains → evaluated. Best LightGBM config trains → evaluated
4. Compare F1The model with higher F1 on the test set wins
5. Save winnerWinning model saved as TICKER_no_sentiment.pkl or TICKER_with_sentiment.pkl. Loser is kept as a backup file

8. Metrics — Full Technical Explanation

All metrics are measured on the test set — data the model was never trained on. This prevents "data leakage" where the model memorises training data but fails on real new data. The test set is always the most recent time period (time-based split).

The Confusion Matrix — Foundation of All Metrics

Every metric below comes from this 2×2 table of what the model predicted vs what actually happened:

Model predicted UPModel predicted DOWN
Actually went UPTrue Positive (TP)False Negative (FN) — missed it
Actually went DOWNFalse Positive (FP) — false alarmTrue Negative (TN)

Accuracy

Formula: (TP + TN) / (TP + TN + FP + FN)

"Out of every prediction made on unseen data, what fraction was correct?"

Example: Model makes 100 predictions → 65 correct → Accuracy = 65%

50% = coin flip (random)  ·  55–60% = meaningful signal  ·  65%+ = strong for stock prediction
Your best result: 71.7% (BABA, 1-month, no sentiment)

Limitation: If 70% of days are UP days, a model that always predicts UP scores 70% accuracy without learning anything. That's why F1 is also used.

Precision

Formula: TP / (TP + FP)

"Of all the times the model shouted UP, how often was it actually right?"

Example: Model predicts UP 40 times → actually went UP 28 times → Precision = 70%

High precision = fewer false alarms. Important if acting on UP predictions (don't want to buy a stock that then drops).

Recall

Formula: TP / (TP + FN)

"Of all the days the stock actually went UP, how many did the model correctly identify?"

Example: Stock went UP on 50 days → model correctly called UP on 35 of them → Recall = 70%

High recall = catches most real UP movements. Low recall = the model misses real opportunities. There's a trade-off: a model can boost recall by predicting UP more aggressively, but that lowers precision.

F1 Score

Formula: 2 × (Precision × Recall) / (Precision + Recall)

"The harmonic mean of Precision and Recall — punishes extreme imbalance between the two."

Example: Precision = 70%, Recall = 70% → F1 = 70%
But: Precision = 100%, Recall = 1% → F1 = only 2% (exposed as useless despite "perfect" precision)

This is why F1 is the tiebreaker in competitive training — the winning model must be good at both finding UPs AND avoiding false alarms.
Your best result: 72.2% (AAPL, 1-week, no sentiment)

Why F1 Is Used as the Competition Tiebreaker

Stock UP/DOWN data is rarely 50/50. In a bull market, 60–65% of days close UP. A lazy model can score 60% accuracy by always predicting UP — but its recall for DOWN days would be 0%. F1 catches this because Precision and Recall for the DOWN class would be terrible, dragging F1 down. F1 forces the model to actually learn both directions.

SHAP Values (Explainability)

SHapley Additive exPlanations — a game-theory method that fairly distributes the "credit" for a prediction across all input features.

How it works: SHAP runs the model many times with different subsets of features to measure how much each feature changes the output. Features that consistently push the prediction toward UP get a positive SHAP value; those pushing toward DOWN get a negative value.

Example output on the site:
  • RSI: 27.4   SHAP: +0.18 → "Oversold reading pushed prediction UP"
  • sentiment_lag1: −0.42   SHAP: −0.09 → "Yesterday's negative news pushed prediction DOWN"
  • macd: −0.003   SHAP: −0.06 → "Weak MACD pushed prediction DOWN"

This is what turns a black-box number into an explanation a non-technical user can understand.

Class Imbalance Handling

Problem: In a rising market, 60–65% of training days are labelled UP. If left uncorrected, the model learns "just always say UP."

Fix in the code: scale_pos_weight = count(DOWN rows) / count(UP rows)
This tells XGBoost to treat each DOWN example as more important, forcing it to learn both directions equally.
LightGBM uses is_unbalance=True which does the same automatically.

10. How Sentiment Works

  1. GNews fetches headlines for each stock every 30 minutes
  2. FinBERT (a BERT model fine-tuned on financial text) scores each headline: −1.0 (very negative) to +1.0 (very positive)
  3. Scores stored in the news_sentiment database table
  4. Rolling averages, standard deviation, and momentum become extra ML features
  5. Breaking news → predictions re-run within 30 minutes via event_predict.py

12. Likely Questions — Quick Answers

Q: Why LightGBM/XGBoost instead of a neural network?

Gradient boosting outperforms neural nets on small tabular datasets. Stock data per ticker is only a few thousand rows — not enough for a deep network to generalise. Tree-based models win here.

Q: Why can't it predict more accurately?

Stock prices are partially random (Efficient Market Hypothesis). 50% is the theoretical floor. Getting consistently above 60% on real unseen data is considered good in academic literature.

Q: Does adding news sentiment improve accuracy?

Sometimes. For short horizons (1d) it can help. For longer horizons (21d, 252d) the no-sentiment model sometimes wins — suggesting that for long-term predictions, price patterns matter more than today's news cycle.

Q: How does the chatbot know what to say?

It doesn't just answer generically. Before every response, the system pulls live data from the database (predictions, sentiment scores, prices, model metrics) and injects it into Claude's context. Claude explains that specific real data in plain English.

Q: Could this be used for real trading?

It's a research and educational tool. Real trading systems need much more: transaction costs, slippage modelling, risk management, regulatory compliance, and much larger datasets. This demonstrates the ML pipeline and explainability concepts.

Q: What is the FNSPID dataset?

Financial News and Stock Price Integration Dataset — a large academic dataset from HuggingFace containing years of financial news headlines paired with stock price movements. Used to pre-train supplementary models.

Q: How is the model accuracy verified?

Standard train/test split — models are trained on historical data up to a cutoff date and evaluated on data they have never seen. Predictions are also stored live in the database and resolved daily (actual UP/DOWN checked against the prediction).

How to Interpret These Results

Why is live accuracy lower than training test-set accuracy?
Training metrics (60–72%) were measured on a historical test set from 2025–early 2026. The live predictions ran during May 2026 — a period of unusual market volatility caused by US-China trade tariff announcements and Federal Reserve rate uncertainty. Models trained on more stable 2023–2024 conditions naturally struggled with this regime shift.

Why do some stocks (BABA, AMD) do well and others (MSFT, META) do poorly?
BABA's price is heavily influenced by Chinese government policy and USD/CNY rate — features the model includes (mchi_return_1d, cny_usd_return, yield_curve). MSFT and META in May 2026 moved on earnings guidance and AI capex news, which is harder to capture from technical indicators alone.

Is 52–54% live accuracy good?
Yes — random guessing is 50%. Any consistent edge above 50% on real unseen market data is meaningful. Academic literature considers 52–55% live accuracy on daily stock direction a strong result. The key word is consistent — this needs more data points (months, not weeks) to be statistically significant.

Sample size caveat: 11 predictions per stock for 1d is a small sample. A single volatile week can swing a stock's score from 70% to 30%. More data over coming months will give a clearer picture.

15. Summary — What This Project Demonstrates

What was built: A fully live, end-to-end AI stock prediction system deployed at chimatest.online. It covers 10 major stocks across 4 time horizons, updates automatically every day, and explains every prediction in plain English through an AI chatbot.

What the results show:
  • Short-term (1-day) prediction is the hardest — averaging ~54% accuracy, just above the 50% random baseline. This is consistent with academic literature on daily stock prediction.
  • Medium-term (1-month) is the most learnable — averaging 69.3% accuracy, with individual models reaching 85.2% (BABA). The model has enough time to find real patterns without being overwhelmed by daily noise.
  • Long-term (1-year, market-relative) averages 72.3% accuracy, showing the model can consistently identify stocks likely to outperform the S&P 500 index.
  • Adding news sentiment sometimes helps, sometimes doesn't — it improves 1-day predictions (54.4% F1 vs 47.2%) but slightly hurts 1-month models. This is a genuine finding: short-term news has more impact on daily moves than on month-long trends.

Technical contributions:
  • Competitive training — LightGBM vs XGBoost with automatic winner selection per stock/horizon
  • Event-driven re-prediction within 30 minutes of breaking news (not next-day)
  • SHAP explainability on every prediction — identifies the top 3 driving features
  • 46-feature input combining price history, 8 technical indicators, 5 market indices, sector ETFs, Chinese market data, earnings surprises, fundamentals, and macroeconomic data
  • FinBERT sentiment pipeline scoring live news headlines every 30 minutes
  • Production deployment: GCP VM + Neon serverless DB + Vercel CDN + automated scheduler

Limitations and future work:
  • Small live sample size (11 predictions per stock) — needs months of data to draw statistically significant conclusions
  • The 21-day and 252-day live accuracy is still pending — results expected June 2026 and May 2027 respectively
  • Could be extended with options data, order flow, or earnings call transcript sentiment
  • Not a trading system — real deployment would require risk management, position sizing, and regulatory compliance
